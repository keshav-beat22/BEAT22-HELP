Yes, they <strong>can still use it.</strong>
If someone bought a <strong>non-exclusive license</strong> for your beat before it was sold under <strong>exclusive rights</strong>, they are still legally allowed to use the material as per the terms of their original license.

Let’s break it down
<h2><strong>Non-Exclusive License: What it means</strong></h2>
A non-exclusive license means:
<ul>
 	<li>The buyer has limited rights to use your beat (for streaming, performances, music videos, etc.)</li>
 	<li>You, as the producer, <strong>retain ownership</strong> and can sell the same license to others — <strong>until</strong> an exclusive sale occurs.</li>
</ul>
<h2><strong>What happens after an Exclusive License is sold?</strong></h2>
<ul>
 	<li>The beat will be <strong>removed</strong> from the platform.</li>
 	<li>However, <strong>previous non-exclusive buyers</strong> can <strong>continue to use</strong> the beat under the original license they purchased.</li>
</ul>
Their rights <strong>remain valid</strong> — nothing changes for them.
<h2><strong>Important for Sellers to know</strong></h2>
<ul>
 	<li>You <strong>cannot retroactively revoke</strong> a non-exclusive license after an exclusive sale.</li>
 	<li>If the exclusive buyer needs full clearance, they should be informed that <strong>previous licenses remain active.</strong></li>
</ul>
<em>Tip: Always keep track of how many licenses you’ve sold before accepting an exclusive deal.</em>
